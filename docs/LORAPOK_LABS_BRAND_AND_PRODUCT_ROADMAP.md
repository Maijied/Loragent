# Lorapok Labs Brand and Product Roadmap

**Status:** Working roadmap  
**Prepared:** 16 October 2026

## Strategic aim

Make Lorapok Labs easier to understand, trust, and explore. Present a coherent studio identity while giving each product enough room to explain its own audience, license, maturity, and technical strengths.

## Current evidence

The Labs portal uses the line “Open-source products that feel alive” and describes its approach through Biological UI, Sensory Computing, and Open Engineering. The founder profile describes a mostly open-source ecosystem of more than 20 products; the portal says 30+; Lorapok AI says 33+. LinkedIn has separate Lorapok Labs company and showcase pages. The company page’s About text says founded in 2023, its structured “Founded” field says 2025, and the [showcase page](https://www.linkedin.com/showcase/lorapok/) lists 2024; the founder profile says 2023. Keep the source-specific dates unresolved until the owner confirms the canonical date. The official site’s Marketplace & Social section and a Facebook page search did not surface a verifiable official Facebook page, so treat Facebook presence as unconfirmed.

The portal currently contains 54 catalog records across 11 categories. The records include product components and creator works, so they do not establish the number of distinct, active Labs products. License claims also vary by project: the portal repository is MIT-licensed, while the Lorapok Player site identifies LL-NC-1.0. Every product needs its own license check.

The full first-pass list is in [the portfolio catalog draft](LORAPOK_LABS_PORTFOLIO_CATALOG_DRAFT.md). It was read from the current, uncommitted portal working tree; treat it as a starting index until each linked repository is checked.

## Positioning direction

**Working description:** Lorapok Labs publishes practical software across developer tools, AI, media, localization, and desktop applications, with an experimental design language informed by biological and sensory ideas.

Keep “Open-source products that feel alive” as the current brand line. Explain what each product does in plain language before using biological metaphors. Describe open-source status and other legal terms per product, according to its repository license.

## Portfolio strategy

Classify every candidate into one of four lifecycle groups:

1. **Maintained products:** current owner, support path, license, release, and install instructions are verified.
2. **Supporting components:** packages, extensions, adapters, or interfaces that belong to a larger product family.
3. **Experiments and creator works:** visible parts of the ecosystem that are not presented as maintained software products.
4. **Archived or unrelated work:** retained in a historical or personal portfolio when appropriate, but excluded from current Labs product totals.

Select the first products to feature only after comparing verified adoption, user need, maintenance capacity, differentiation, release readiness, and cross-project dependencies. Do not infer priority from the current “featured” flag alone.

## Delivery roadmap

### Weeks 1–2: Establish the facts

- Verify the 54 portal records against their repositories; resolve product families, aliases, contributor work, archived projects, and Labs ownership.
- Confirm each license, current release, working product link, supported platform, and maintenance status.
- Resolve the founding year and public product-count language with the founder.
- Create a dated claims ledger for metrics, performance, privacy, compatibility, and licensing.

**Exit gate:** no entry is labeled a current Labs product without a repository, owner relationship, status, license, and source date.

### Weeks 3–4: Set the brand system

- Confirm the primary audience and the three most important jobs Lorapok products help with.
- Approve a short company description, product description template, voice guide, and terms for the four lifecycle groups.
- Document shared Labs design tokens and accessibility expectations separately from product-specific themes.
- Align public copy across the Labs portal, founder profile, and selected product sites using the claims ledger.

**Exit gate:** every public Labs claim has an evidence link or is explicitly framed as a goal.

### Weeks 5–8: Improve the selected product pages

- Choose a small first group based on the verified scoring criteria.
- Give each selected product a consistent page structure: user problem, current capability, supported platforms, install path, repository, license, release status, and support link.
- Repair stale links and remove unsupported performance or ecosystem-count claims.
- Use real product screenshots and approved assets; keep concept art separate from interface evidence.

**Exit gate:** selected pages describe shippable behavior and have working source, license, and installation links.

### Weeks 9–12: Prove one ecosystem connection

- Choose one integration opportunity only after confirming that both products are maintained, compatible, and useful together.
- Build a small, measurable integration or shared workflow; publish a short technical note with setup steps and limits.
- Review adoption and maintenance cost before expanding the connection to more products.

**Exit gate:** continue only if the integration solves a user problem and can be maintained within available capacity.

## Measures

- Every catalog record has a verified ownership relationship, lifecycle group, license, repository, and review date.
- No public product count is published without a deduplicated definition and an “as of” date.
- Every performance, privacy, compatibility, and download claim links to evidence and an observation date.
- Every featured product has working install, source, license, and support links.
- Shared visual rules pass keyboard, contrast, responsive, and reduced-motion review.
- Integration work has a stated user problem, owner, and ongoing maintenance cost.

## Decisions to resolve with the founder

- Which audience should lead the company story: developers, creators, or everyday app users?
- Which products have the highest real usage and the strongest current maintenance capacity?
- What founding year and distinct-product counting rule should be canonical?
- Which work belongs in the Labs portfolio versus personal, contributor, or historical collections?
- Which Figma file should provide the approved brand and component source?
